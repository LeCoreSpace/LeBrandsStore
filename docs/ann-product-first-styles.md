# Product-first Ann styles

## Goal and limits

Design for a visitor arriving directly from an advertisement at a product page.
The visitor should be able to understand the product, make a selection and find
the purchasing action without first exploring the homepage. Home, collection,
about, contact, cart and checkout should still form a coherent store.

The additional styles are Social Pop, Creator Feed and Proof & Detail. These
are design hypotheses, not claims of higher conversion or a guarantee that
every member of an age group prefers the same aesthetic.

## Research used

Reviewed 2026-10-15:

- [Baymard: Product Page UX 2026](https://baymard.com/research-articles/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux).
  Its US/European benchmark reports mediocre-or-worse mobile product-page UX at
  62% of the benchmarked sites. Relevant recommendations include visible size
  selection, in-scale and on-model photography, and accessible shipping/return
  information. This is usability evidence, not a forecast of this app's conversion.
- [Baymard: Payment UX](https://baymard.com/learn/payment-ux).
  Clear order totals, predictable next-step labels and low-distraction checkout
  reduce uncertainty. A theme must not invent prices, supported payment methods
  or new checkout capabilities to imitate the appearance of another store.
- [DHL: Social Commerce Trends](https://www.dhl.com/global-en/microsites/ec/ecommerce-insights/insights/reports/social-commerce-trends.html).
  Social discovery, customer reviews and creator content inform the visual
  directions. The source explicitly limits the statistical significance of
  subgroup comparisons; generational preferences are directional.
- [Rhode product example](https://www.rhodeskin.com/products/peptide-lip-tint-toast)
  and [Starface product example](https://starface.world/products/hydro-stars-refill).
  Inspected as contrasting examples of product media, product detail and visual
  identity. Their brand, artwork, photographs, reviews, claims, subscriptions,
  code and business terms are not copied. Their business success does not
  demonstrate that a particular layout caused it.

## Distinct design hypotheses

| Style | Presentation | Language | Purchase-decision emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Social Pop | Playful colour panels, bold media and rounded components | Upbeat, short and welcoming | Quickly identify the product and explore its actual details |
| Creator Feed | Photo sequences and real-use context | Approachable, conversational captions | Picture the product in everyday use without mistaking sample imagery for customer testimony |
| Proof & Detail | Structured information, precise hierarchy and explanatory panels | Calm, specific and clear | Read specifications, materials or usage information actually supplied by the merchant |

Each style changes the whole store composition, not just the font or accent.
Existing five styles remain supported.

## Content and safety boundaries

- The selected subcategory owns the image pack and sample catalogue, not the
  style. Switching styles must not change sample prices or product identity.
- Reuse the five existing image packs. No new image or video generation is
  required for these designs. Photos must not be disguised as playable video.
- Contemporary English is a tone choice, not a new translation feature. Avoid
  forced slang, demographic stereotypes, exclusionary language or purchase
  pressure aimed at children. Gen Alpha includes children and parent purchasers.
- Actual product names, descriptions, prices, stock and media remain catalogue
  data. Style text surrounds those facts rather than overwriting them.
- No fictional real-store reviews, medical evidence, before/after results,
  certifications, countdowns, discounts or shipping promises.
- Bundles and cross-selling must use supported catalogue and pricing behavior.
  A bundle-looking composition does not implement a discount or a subscription.
- Keep the existing guarded cart, checkout and publication flow. A style preview
  never places an order, charges a customer, publishes a store or imports demo
  inventory.
- Accessibility, keyboard operation, reduced motion and mobile readability
  take precedence over decorative animation.

## Evaluation after release

Useful future measurements are product-view to add-to-cart rate, checkout
starts, completed purchases, returns, mobile performance and accessibility.
Compare similar traffic, products and offers; do not attribute changes to the
theme without a suitable experiment.
